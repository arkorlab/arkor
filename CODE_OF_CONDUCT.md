# Code of Conduct

Arkor is built in the open, with and for people who want TypeScript-native fine-tuning to exist. This document describes how we treat each other in every Arkor community space: this GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions), the [Discord server](https://discord.arkor.ai), and any official account or event where someone represents the project.

It has two parts: **how we behave** (the conduct baseline) and **how we collaborate** (etiquette for issues, pull requests, and talking to maintainers). Both are binding.

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and maintainers pledge to make participation in the Arkor community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Being kind and patient, especially with first-time contributors and non-native English speakers.
- Assuming good faith, and keeping technical disagreement technical.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Accepting responsibility, apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience.
- Focusing on what is best for the community and the project, not just for ourselves.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- Harassment of any kind, public or private, including sexualized language or imagery and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Publishing others' private information (physical or email address, etc.) without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Collaboration etiquette

These rules exist so that effort is not wasted and nobody's work is stepped on. They apply to everyone, including maintainers.

### Working on issues

- **Claim before you code.** If you want to work on an issue, comment on it (a short "I'd like to work on this" is enough) and wait for a maintainer to assign it to you. Outside collaborators cannot self-assign on GitHub, so the comment is the claim; we try to respond quickly.
- **An assigned issue is taken.** Do not start working on, or open a PR for, an issue that is assigned to someone else. "I finished it faster" does not make a competing PR welcome.
- **Stalled is not abandoned.** If an assigned issue has had no visible activity for about two weeks, ask politely on the issue whether the assignee is still working on it. Maintainers may then unassign and free it up. Only maintainers make that call; do not treat silence as permission.
- **It's fine to let go.** If you cannot continue, say so on the issue so it can be reassigned. Dropping an issue is completely normal and never counts against you.
- **Finish before you stockpile.** Prefer completing your current issue before claiming several more. Claiming issues you are not actively working on blocks others.

### Pull requests

- **One issue, one PR, no duplicates.** Before opening a PR, search open PRs for the same issue or change. If one already exists, contribute there (review it, comment, test it) instead of opening a competing one. Duplicate PRs will be closed in favor of the earliest active one.
- **Different approach? Discuss first.** If you believe an existing PR is taking the wrong direction, explain your alternative on the issue or that PR and let a maintainer weigh in. Do not silently race it with your own PR.
- **Link the issue.** Reference the issue your PR addresses (`Closes #123`) so assignment and duplicate checks stay easy.
- **Unsolicited rewrites of someone's PR belong in review.** Suggest changes via review comments or a suggestion diff on their PR, not by re-submitting their work under your name.

For everything about PR quality (size, tests, descriptions), see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md): we err on the side of accepting PRs, and none of these rules are meant to scare you away from opening one.

### Communicating with maintainers and contributors

Maintainers and contributors are people volunteering their time. They are collaborators, not your personal support desk, and not obligated to be your friends.

- **Keep it public and on-topic.** Ask questions in issues, discussions, or the public Discord channels. Do not DM maintainers or contributors for code review, merge requests, assignment requests, or general support unless they invited you to.
- **No spam, no nagging.** Do not ping the same person repeatedly, post "any update?" daily, or drop unrelated chat, self-promotion, or job requests into project spaces. If nothing has happened for a week or so, one polite bump in the relevant thread is fine.
- **Use reactions, not "+1" comments.** A thumbs-up reaction signals interest without generating notification noise for everyone subscribed.
- **Reviews take time.** An unanswered PR usually means "busy", not "rejected". See the bug-report section of [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for the same principle in the other direction: most stalled threads are waiting on information, not ignoring you.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing this Code of Conduct, and will take fair corrective action in response to any behavior they consider inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Depending on severity and history, that action ranges from a private reminder of these rules, through removal of comments, unassignment of issues, or closing of PRs, up to temporary or permanent bans from project spaces. The maintainers' call is final.

To report a violation, email **conduct@arkor.ai**. For anything security-related, use security@arkor.ai instead (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)). All reports will be reviewed promptly and handled with as much confidentiality as the situation allows. Maintainers who do not enforce this Code of Conduct in good faith face the same consequences as anyone else.
